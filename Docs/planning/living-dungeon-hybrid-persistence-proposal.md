## 1. Recommendation and approval boundary

Retain a bounded set of privately owned `RunExecution` instances between foreground pumps. Advance intermediate party state deterministically in memory; publish a complete canonical save at configured checkpoints and mandatory barriers. Keep the existing canonical wallet, run history, scheduler and atomic writer. Do not add a writable run journal, general cache, worker, ECS/Jobs/Burst dependency or persistence service.

The owner approves **exact recovery of durably accepted time/work, with bounded rollback of uncommitted activity**. Newly observed foreground time is provisional until its acceptance and coverage anchor are durably published. After an unexpected termination, recover accepted work first; classify the remaining valid wall-clock gap as **unknown absence processed under offline restrictions**, without asserting that the process was backgrounded throughout it. This may reproduce ordinary activity differently from the lost foreground suffix. It cannot erase or repeat confirmed permanent effects. Before O1, such a gap uses only the existing passive-offline policy; no absent-time adventurer catch-up is implemented or promised.

This policy requires a reviewed versioned time-provenance representation. Existing schema 15 party checkpoints can represent execution prefixes, but existing fields alone cannot reliably distinguish accepted foreground coverage, generic save time and consumed offline time. Do not silently repurpose them or select a new schema number here.

### Owner-approved policy direction (2026-10-24)

The owner explicitly approved the following policy decisions, which are recorded here without authorizing implementation:

1. **Bounded provisional rollback:** recent unconfirmed movement, combat and carried loot may roll back after unexpected termination within separately approved bounds. Durably accepted work must recover exactly; confirmed construction, resources, research transactions, settlements and rewards never lose durability or duplicate.
2. **Unknown crash absence:** retain existing passive offline mana behavior before O1. Once exact offline adventurer catch-up (O1) is implemented and qualified, treat valid unaccounted post-crash time under the offline-party restrictions without claiming the app was definitely backgrounded. Do not create offline-party obligations during H1/H2, double-credit time or bypass the versioned W/`lastSavedUtcUnix` mapping gate.
3. **Known foreground persistence failure:** freeze new authoritative progression prospectively at a safe complete boundary, retain previously accepted work, present a localized pending/recovery state and allow explicit retry after proven rollback or recovery after uncertain authority. Do not silently classify the known stall as offline or accumulate unbounded unsaved progress. No new offline mana is granted solely for that blocked foreground interval.

These approvals establish **policy**, not the technical cut/acknowledgement implementation. Sections 4–14 retain explicit gates for timestamp precision, coverage/legacy mapping, checkpoint cadence, memory/work bounds, compatibility, migration, UI details and test qualification. The revised existing design locks and specifications in section 12 remain **proposed wording**, not enacted amendments. Until separately qualified and implemented, existing per-pump publication, committed-only live presentation and historical readers remain binding. Neither the historical 30-second save interval nor current pump limits select the new rollback window.

## 3. Durable, provisional and transient facts

The word **committed** in `RunExecution`/codec internals means an event has entered that execution's deterministic prefix. In this proposal, **durably committed** means independently validated canonical bytes passed the durable protocol, or were independently recovered as authoritative on reopen. An in-memory prefix digest alone has no durable authority.

| Fact | Durable authority | Permitted private state | Required publication boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Layout, construction, assignments/custody, purchases, research transactions, claimed rewards, unlocks | Existing canonical owners | Candidate/quote only; no confirmed success | Atomic player transaction after earlier due work closes |
| Total mana/permanent resources and protected obligations | Existing wallet and reserve derivation | Detached proposed tick/debit/grant; never a spendable second balance | Every applicable permanent-effect timestamp/transaction |
| Persistent monster HP/revival and recovery debits | `monsterContinuity` with spatial/custody ownership | Run-local combat HP/receipts; candidate net-damage reconciliation | Existing settlement/recovery consequence boundary; no new per-hit canonical injury policy |
| Run ID/sequence, admitted start inputs and admission time | `runHistory.NextRunSequence` plus canonical party record | Preparation/probe before reservation; hidden newborn candidate | Reservation and allocator advance together before an admitted party is exposed or advances in normal play |
| Party movement, members' run-local HP/deaths, route/decisions, carried abstract loot, nonterminal combat prefix | Canonical party checkpoint at last publication | Same execution may advance ahead within approved limits | Periodic/limit checkpoint or any earlier mandatory barrier |
| Occupied floor, waiting blocker/deadline, completed ordinary transfer | Authenticated checkpoint and cross-party scheduler validation | Private occupancy map/control transitions | Bounded checkpoint; earlier if a permanent action relies on that vacancy/occupancy |
| Previously blocked release | Exact terminal/retirement vacancy cause and release authorization | Transaction-local release plus completed transfer | Same atomic timestamp as its causal settlement; never persist Released alone |
| Scheduler horizon, opportunity ordinal/time and consumed opportunities | `livingDungeon.Scheduler` | One private successor; opportunity not allocating a party can remain provisional | Checkpoint; identity allocation is mandatory |
| Accepted time/interval and processing frontier | Scheduler plus proposed provenance fields in section 9 | Newly sampled time, uncommitted event work | Acceptance must publish before it is called durably accepted |
| Heat, survivor knowledge, objectives, history, retirement | Existing canonical consequence/history owners | Verified but unpublished terminal candidate | Complete terminal timestamp, exactly once |
| Animation, marker interpolation, camera, effects, label placement, view selection | None | Transient detached observer | None; never changes simulation, wallet or history |

There is one serialized scheduler and one canonical write path. A retained execution is a private working successor of a specific canonical session, never a save owner. A UI projection is a copy, never a mutable execution handle. The separate nonauthoritative editor draft store remains governed by INV-12; it cannot hold party recovery authority.

## 4. Foreground time provenance and recovery policy

### 4.1 Frontiers and coverage

Use the following contract names; these are not final C# identifiers:

| Symbol/fact | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `C` | Last **durably closed** complete logical timestamp. All due obligations through C are in canonical state. |
| `A` | Durably accepted logical horizon in the same canonical revision. `C <= A`; `(C, A]` must be processed exactly, even after interruption. Accepting elapsed work is not completing it. |
| `P` | Latest privately closed complete timestamp in the current working successor. It may extend beyond A using provisional observations. No public permanent effect follows P until publication. |
| `O` | Latest eligible foreground observation in this process. It is a bounded scalar horizon with paired coverage sample, not a list of frame samples and not durable acceptance. |
| `W` | Proposed last **durably accounted wall-clock endpoint**, paired with explicit coverage classification and the relevant accepted logical horizon. It is not automatically the start of an offline grant and cannot advance merely because a file was saved. |
| `lastSavedUtcUnix` | Existing canonical field used by the current passive offline mana resolver as its source timestamp. Current canonical foreground saves can also advance it. It therefore represents an existing passive-time coverage boundary, not simply the physical time of the last disk write and not proof that a mana grant occurred. |
| Pending interval | An accepted interval's immutable identity, time class, endpoints, captured boundary inputs and processing frontier. H1/H2 may retain only obligations their supported implementation can process. Durable offline-party interval obligations begin only with separately implemented and qualified O1 behavior. |

Within a foreground segment, `C <= P <= O` and `C <= A <= O`; P may be below A while draining or above A provisionally. Event ordinals/party logical times must agree with the relevant closed prefix. A durable candidate publishes a frontier `C'` and accepted `A' >= C'`, with exact checkpoints, passive tick state and coverage for everything through A'. Advancing A beyond C is optional, bounded by the pending-work policy; shrinking a previously accepted A is forbidden. Do not move C through a partial equal-time boundary.

Foreground durations come from eligible monotonic deltas, never UTC subtraction. Capture a wall endpoint alongside the **observation/acceptance cut**, not at the end of potentially slow serialization. A later flush finishing older work retains that endpoint. Restart/focus transitions reanchor the monotonic source; a previous process's absolute counter value has no meaning. UTC provenance is local evidence under existing validity/anomaly rules, not trusted backend time or clock-cheat prevention.

The proposed W and existing `lastSavedUtcUnix` must have one reviewed relationship. They must not become independent elapsed-time authorities. Recommend a single canonical accounted cut, with the passive resolver's source timestamp and W either representing that same cut or having an explicit validated versioned mapping when precision or historical compatibility requires a distinction. The exact field representation, timestamp conversion, remainder handling and legacy transition mechanics remain a **schema-design and owner-approval gate**. This proposal does not establish that mapping or claim the current code already enforces it.

The approved implementation must enforce these invariants:

1. **Coverage is not a grant.** Advancing a boundary because foreground time has been accepted must prevent that covered time from later receiving passive offline credit, but must not itself issue an offline mana grant.
2. **Acceptance is not completion.** W may cover foreground time accepted through A while C remains earlier. That accepted work must still drain exactly. A newer W or `lastSavedUtcUnix` cannot erase `(C, A]`.
3. **One passive interval, one consumption.** An actual offline mana grant and the advancement of its consumed source boundary must publish atomically with the resulting canonical wallet. W, any consumption evidence and `lastSavedUtcUnix` must agree with that transition under the reviewed mapping.
4. **No alternate grant origin.** The current passive resolver consumes `lastSavedUtcUnix`. H1/H2 must not independently calculate another grant from W, pause time, reopen time or an interval descriptor.
5. **No save-time leap over unhandled absence.** A generic canonical save, action save or checkpoint finishing later must not move the passive coverage boundary past a valid unconsumed offline interval merely by recording current UTC. The supported lifecycle/time transition must resolve that interval first or preserve its existing boundary.
6. **No double foreground/offline coverage.** Wall coverage paired with durably accepted foreground work cannot also become an offline interval. Genuinely background/closed absence, accepted foreground work and any explicitly approved nonaccruing technical suspension must remain distinguishable.
7. **No callback inference.** Pause/resume fields are evidence only when included in independently proven canonical state. Recovery cannot assume a pause or termination callback ran.
8. **No interrupted-write cursor advance.** Candidate preparation does not advance live coverage or consume an interval. Proven rollback retains the predecessor's wallet, horizons and time boundaries together. A recovered authoritative candidate retains all of them together, even if the former process never acknowledged success. Ambiguous authority blocks new credit rather than selecting whichever cursor is newer.

Current canonical foreground saves and passive offline grants both affect `lastSavedUtcUnix`; integrating W therefore requires reviewing all relevant save paths, not just adding a scheduler field. H1 must demonstrate the transition through ordinary foreground saves, action saves, accepted-but-unclosed draining, pause, resume, initial load and interrupted offline-grant publication before activating the new interpretation.

At a controlled pause/focus-loss transition, sample the final eligible foreground cut and preserve its paired boundary while bounded accepted work drains. A suspended pump must not move that boundary into the later background interval. Resume must retain the genuine background interval for the existing passive offline policy and must not replay it as active ticks. If the final foreground cut cannot be durably published, recovery uses only proven predecessor/candidate authority; it does not fabricate a successful pause boundary.

Foreground coverage must exclude known focus-loss/background intervals. Record each transition needed by still-pending work before compacting it; do not merge across different eligibility classes. When valid monotonic duration and wall samples disagree, never silently convert the discrepancy into extra foreground time. Invalid/backward/discontinuous wall samples cannot advance W or accept a new wall-based offline grant; retain the last proven endpoint, flag time recovery/anomaly and block ambiguous credit until the established time policy can resolve it. Monotonic observations may remain provisional only within their bounds. Selecting quantization and anomaly treatment is an approval gate (section 11).

### 4.2 Unexpected termination: recommended rule

1. Use the existing qualified canonical load/recovery path to establish one trusted complete save. Reject ambiguous authority before applying new consequences. At the **defined recovery-entry observation cut**, capture a valid, stable reopen wall-clock endpoint `R` **once, before potentially lengthy accepted-work draining**. Establish the trust/anomaly status of that sample without granting credit. If no valid observation is available, block ambiguous new time credit under the established policy; do not invent `R`.
2. Restore its exact party/checkpoint/executable state and `C`, `A`, the supported provenance representation and the existing passive-time boundary. Drain `(C, A]` in bounded event batches using its saved supported time class, inputs and eligibility. Do not reissue ticks or settlements already inside C. Preserve A while making progress. This draining must not advance `R` or extend the pre-reopen absence interval.
3. Acknowledge no lost private suffix. Private events/time after the durable cut disappear. They did not spend resources, settle history or authorize a permanent effect.
4. Distinguish the following time classes at the stable recovery cut:

   | Time class | Recovery meaning |
   | --- | --- |
   | Proven background/closed interval | Known absence eligible for the currently implemented passive offline rules; later party simulation requires O1. |
   | Unknown post-crash absence | Time whose actual foreground/background split cannot be reconstructed from durable evidence. **Owner-approved direction:** existing passive offline handling before O1; eligible offline-restricted party processing only after O1 is implemented and qualified, under the reviewed W/passive-boundary mapping. Never assert definitively background or foreground. |
   | Known foreground persistence stall | Time during which the running process knows the application remained foregrounded. It is not background/closed absence and must not silently receive an offline classification. Its accrual/suspension policy requires explicit approval. |

5. Resolve passive offline mana using the **existing canonical source boundary and reviewed W relationship**, not an independent W→R calculation. The future passive-mana path must use or reconcile the **same stable `R` captured at recovery entry**; it must not resample UTC after a long drain and silently count that processing time as pre-reopen absence. Finish earlier accepted foreground work before calculating state-dependent passive boundary inputs. Publish any grant, consumed boundary and corresponding provenance atomically. On failed acceptance/publication, retain the original proven authority and issue no successful-looking grant. Time elapsed after `R` during recovery requires its own separately approved foreground/suspension handling.
6. **Before O1:** record only descriptive provenance that the supported application can safely interpret. Do not create a durable promise to simulate offline party events, advance the adventurer horizon for absent time, or queue an interval that H1/H2 cannot process. After supported accepted foreground work drains and the existing passive offline transition succeeds, normal foreground gameplay resumes. There is no wait for a future offline-party implementation.
7. **After O1 is separately implemented and qualified:** durably accept eligible offline-party intervals with fixed endpoints, captured inputs and bounded resumable processing state before treating them as accepted obligations. Preserve each accepted remainder across interruption. An application unable to execute an already accepted supported obligation must fail closed or use its reviewed compatibility path; it cannot silently discard it.

For the future O1 policy, the unaccounted post-crash interval may be described as W→R only after the schema design proves what W covers and how it relates to the consumed passive boundary. This notation is not permission to grant mana from W independently or to skip already accepted foreground work.

Intervals use one consistent endpoint convention, so the event at a transition belongs to exactly one class. Recommend completing due work at the foreground cut under foreground rules, then applying the supported absence policy to later wall time. The versioned mapping must explicitly carry unit/precision and any fractional remainder; never reconstruct a remainder from render frames. Exact transition mechanics remain a schema-design gate. R is a captured recovery-entry endpoint, not a promise that offline party catch-up has occurred.

Example without invented tuning: a save closes C and accepts a later A with paired wall coverage. The process privately reaches P beyond A, then dies. Reopen captures stable `R` at entry, resumes exactly C→A and discards P's suffix. Existing passive offline rules handle only the remaining eligible interval established from their canonical source boundary through the supported stable endpoint. Before O1, the adventurer timeline does not advance through that absence. After O1, separately accepted eligible absence may advance parties under its approved restrictions. If a terminal timestamp had already published, its history/retirement is in C and its effects are never replayed. If it had only been prepared, neither result nor reward was confirmed.

### 4.3 What rollback means, and the stricter alternative

The approved bound would limit **uncommitted eligible elapsed activity**, not total wall time while an app cannot write. No confirmed player transaction may be rolled back under this allowance.

Before O1, lost provisional foreground combat/loot is discarded back to the proven party prefix; it is not automatically recomputed as offline activity. Existing passive offline mana behavior remains applicable under its established boundary rules. Recording the crash gap does not turn it into an offline-party obligation.

**Owner-approved future O1 direction:** after O1 is implemented and qualified, eligible valid unknown post-crash absence is processed under offline restrictions using a reviewed W/passive-boundary mapping. It is not guaranteed to reproduce the lost provisional foreground outcome or tier crossing. Schema, causal attribution, interval eligibility, corruption/anomaly handling and O1 acceptance tests remain separate implementation gates.

Exact recovery of **every observed foreground millisecond** requires durable acceptance before each observation is relied upon, or another reviewed durable record protocol. A process can die between observation and publication; no termination callback or in-memory cache closes that gap. Even that stronger design cannot know unsampled time precisely. It would retain frequent durable writes and conflict with the proposed batching benefit. **The owner has approved bounded provisional rollback**, with exactness guaranteed for durably accepted work. The maximum rollback exposure, work/memory bounds and recovery encoding have **not** been approved.

Known lifecycle transitions should flush and record coverage before suspension, but correctness must also hold if none of `OnPause`, `OnDisable` or `OnApplicationQuit` runs.

**Owner-approved foreground-stall policy:** when persistence cannot proceed safely, freeze new authoritative simulation prospectively at a proven complete boundary, retaining all prior durably accepted work. Do not silently reclassify the known foreground stall as offline. Present localized pending/retry or recovery-required feedback; after a proven rollback retry only through an explicit action, and after uncertain authority require independent recovery. While blocked, neither add unbounded unsaved adventurer activity nor issue passive offline credit merely for the foreground stall. The application remains physically foregrounded. The exact suspension-cut representation, time-source reanchor, retry UX and bounded-work constraints require H1/H2 design and validation; this approval does not authorize retroactive time reclassification.

Do not retroactively relabel already observed or durably accepted foreground work as suspended or offline to fit a budget. Preserve accepted work and any already established genuine background interval. Recovery after an actual crash may no longer know the full classification of an uncommitted suffix; that uncertainty follows the separately approved crash-gap rule, not an assertion that known foreground stalls are offline.

H1/H2 must not introduce an unprocessable offline-party backlog or require O1 before normal foreground play can resume. Genuine storage failure or ambiguous canonical authority can still require a bounded pending/recovery state and prevent confirmed canonical actions. The proposal does not promise continued authoritative progression while durable storage remains unavailable.

## 5. Checkpoint triggers, hard bounds and failure behavior

Publish the earliest applicable mandatory barrier or approved bounded checkpoint. Coalesce complete timestamps only while no mandatory permanent consequence occurs. Each candidate covers the scheduler and **all** parties consistently, rather than independently saving a selected party.

| Trigger | Required behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Terminal/permanent-effect timestamp | Stop after completing that timestamp's ordered work; publish immediately before processing later timestamps or showing confirmed effects. Multiple same-time effects may share one atomic replacement. |
| Passive tick | Existing permanent mana and applicable research obligations force a barrier whenever their durable state changes. Treat tick completion metadata with its effects atomically; do not use private balance in player spending/admission across an uncommitted barrier. This may limit write reduction substantially. |
| Canonical player edit/resource/research/reward action | Close earlier due work through the action cut, publish needed checkpoint, then validate/commit the action through the canonical writer. An active party is not itself an edit prohibition. A failed earlier barrier leaves the action pending/unconfirmed. |
| Admission | Validate start/lifetime affordability, reserve run identity and immutable start with allocator advance atomically. Do not show/start a newborn before success. Include its required zero-time work and whole timestamp closure. |
| Maximum provisional elapsed age | Before exceeding configured eligible-time exposure, checkpoint a supported complete prefix. Track both unaccepted observations and private processed age; a limit on one cannot leave the other unbounded. |
| Maximum accumulated logical work | Count event/control work, combat/planning/lookahead work and pending accepted work independently of wall time. Check headroom before advancing an indivisible operation. |
| Retained-memory/storage bound | Bound executions, captured snapshots/routes, receipts, observer data, working candidate/rollback copies, interval records and serialized bytes, both per party and aggregate. Reserve checkpointability before admission/advance. Existing serialized-byte bounds alone do not bound heap usage. |
| Pause/focus loss/ordinary close | Freeze eligibility at the final sampled cut and attempt a bounded flush/coverage transition. Drain accepted work before resume. A failed or interrupted flush retains the last proven save; no guaranteed final callback. |
| Stale predecessor, invalid checkpoint, uncertain durable result | Freeze the entire writer/working successor. Reopen/recover before further canonical actions; never continue from an assumed successful state. |

All new limits belong to a validated versioned technical policy/configuration, injected into the owner; gameplay pacing remains in its existing content configuration. Required policy inputs include maximum unaccepted elapsed age, maximum private processed age/work, maximum accepted pending work, retained-state/interval budgets, per-pump work, synchronous flush work and latency warning/deadline policy. **No numeric values are selected here.** Approve and qualify values before enabling hybrid batching. The old 30-second periodic save is neither the new rollback budget nor permission to defer mandatory effects.

Work bounds must be checked before speculative advancement; trigger early with enough reserved capacity to finish the largest admitted complete timestamp and export it. If an equal-time boundary or export cannot fit, reject that advance/admission from the previous proven prefix. Never split a permanent timestamp or enlarge its limit to escape failure.

A time budget is a yield/stop condition between bounded operations, not a guarantee that an OS atomic write can be cancelled. Existing synchronous filesystem calls can outlast a deadline. Preflight bounded serialization/validation/maximum event work, allow at most one in-flight canonical candidate, and freeze new work while it finishes. Do not spawn workers or concurrent writes to hide the stall. A hard wall-clock upper bound on that synchronous protocol is **not proven** and must not be advertised.

If preparation exceeds the allowed budget before replacement, abandon the candidate and retain/reconstruct from the durable predecessor; already accepted horizons remain pending. On proven write rollback, discard the speculative successor and restore the canonical prefix; retry only at an explicit action/lifecycle/recovery request, preserving the current no-per-frame-storage-retry behavior. On uncertain write/readback/rollback, latch recovery-required and preserve evidence. Do not treat timeout as success, roll back a proven committed candidate blindly, keep simulating into unlimited memory, or discard durably accepted intervals.

Preserve genuinely established pause/background boundaries when possible. A persistence stall while the application remains foregrounded does not itself create an offline interval. Stop new progression or acceptance at the approved safe foreground-stall cut in section 4.3; do not retroactively change accepted time classification. Before O1, no failure or recovery path may create an offline-party obligation that the application cannot process. Keep editing drafts and reading confirmed Analysis available where safe, while canonical commits wait for recovery.

## 6. In-memory execution ownership

Existing `RunExecution` can remain alive between calls **as a private single-caller object**: it already retains iterator, party, traversal, kernel, pending announcement, accepted deadline, receipts and transfer controls. That is a feasibility conclusion from source, not qualification of a cross-pump owner. `NextDueTime` can perform lookahead and charge work; it is not a pure query. Failure/`Abandon` poisons the execution, and `TakeOutcome` consumes it. There is no general clone/rewind operation.

Recommend one bounded working set inside the existing foreground `SaveService` coordination, driven only by the serialized `GameRoot`/writer lane. The writer still produces and publishes canonical transactions. Do not make execution retention part of `DetachedCanonicalSaveSession`: its immutable bytes remain the durable predecessor, while exposed validation graphs are not safe global immutable templates.

| Lifecycle/interaction | Contract |
| --- | --- |

Release lineage is a particular integration risk: today its permits bind exact staged predecessor bytes and cannot survive arbitrary mutation or pumps. Retention must preserve the **same logical proof**, rebuilding detached authorization at publication from the proven predecessor and ordered private terminal transitions. Existing `Retired`, `Release` and `ReleasePermit` are not directly reusable capabilities across pumps. A self-consistent replayed checkpoint never proves the canonical blocker was retired. Keep terminal settlement, causal release, completed transfer and closure in one durable timestamp, so no cross-checkpoint vacancy capability needs a new durable owner.

## 7. Permanent barriers and exactly-once order

Preserve the two-floor contract's existing phases, including the distinction between an ordinary transfer attempt and release of a previously blocked party:

1. Apply due configured passive tick obligations.
2. Process existing party events in absolute deadline, numeric RunSequence, local ordinal order. Immediately compose each terminal's verified consequences/history/retirement in the private timestamp candidate.
3. Derive actual vacancy and perform eligible current active-floor monster recovery.
4. Authenticate blocked releases against those terminal causes; complete transfer and enabled same-time work/recovery until stable.
5. Consume the admission opportunity; atomically reserve any admitted identity and process newborn zero-time work plus required consequences.
6. Close the timestamp only when all equal-time work finishes. If any mandatory consequence/reservation occurred, publish now, before later timestamps or acknowledgement.

“Immediate persistence” means that complete timestamp is the next publication and cannot be bypassed; it does not mean publishing a partial timestamp between steps 2 and 3. Multiple private `Stage`/authorization operations can belong to one transaction. Ordinary movement/combat/waiting may span private pumps until a bound or mandatory phase forces publication. Completed ordinary transfers without permanent effects may also remain provisional, but any permanent transaction relying on their occupancy must include/prove that prefix first.

Exactly-once claims apply to canonical transitions, including a recovered commit whose old process never observed success:

- Validate terminal evidence against the unconsumed pinned prefix and reserved ID. Apply verified net monster damage to then-current ownership once, never recreate retired identities. Retain the current C2 readiness/revival and overlapping-party reconciliation rules.
- Mana debits/grants, Heat/cooling, eligible knowledge/objectives, history insertion and retirement publish together with the party/scheduler prefix that caused them. Preserve both foreground Heat/cooling stages. Any future mid-run **permanent** reward/effect becomes a new mandatory barrier; intermediate carried world loot is not a mana grant.
- Tick count/frontier advances with tick effects. Recovery uses currently active, assigned, proven-vacant floors; cost/HP/revival changes publish atomically, including condition-only changes obscured by floating-point wallet rounding.
- Run allocation and party creation are one transition. A failed hidden candidate allocates no acknowledged identity; an allocated durable identity is never reused after a crash. Do not decrement the allocator to reconstruct a lost provisional party.
- Blocked release uses the exact blocker/terminal/vacancy evidence and completes its transfer in the causal transaction. Duplicate notifications/retries cannot add ordinals or another release.
- On mandatory-barrier failure, canonical runtime remains the proven predecessor. Invalidate speculative UI state and consumed executions. Show pending/retry/recovery, never success, completed Analysis or a spendable reward. A caller ignoring a Boolean persistence result violates this contract even if the file writer itself is atomic.

Additional backup retention would need a separately reviewed storage policy; no such change is selected here.

## 9. Save representation and migration assessment

Execution retention and copied live views alone require no new durable field. Current checkpoints already carry immutable start inputs, timing, executable contract, ordinal, deadline, terminal/consumed state, evidence digest/inspection and transfer control. `livingDungeon` already owns accepted/closed horizons, opportunity identity and party pins; the history owner owns allocation and bounded completed outcomes. Reuse those facts, rather than add competing cursors.

The **full recommended time/recovery contract does require additional durable information**. `AcceptedThrough`/`ClosedThrough` are logical units without wall/class provenance; `lastSavedUtcUnix` can change on canonical foreground saves and offline grants; pause/resume fields cannot prove a callback ran or identify all pending intervals. A checkpoint hash and version do not encode an accepted absence or historical economy inputs.

The current passive offline resolver uses `lastSavedUtcUnix` as its source boundary. Adding W must not create another independently advancing passive-time cursor. Recommend one canonical accounted cut, with a strict relationship between W, `lastSavedUtcUnix`, foreground acceptance and any consumed passive grant. Whether that relationship is equality at the current timestamp precision, a validated alias, or an explicit versioned mapping remains a **schema-design gate**. It is not resolved by this proposal.

- How canonical foreground saves advance the paired coverage cut while preserving any accepted-but-unclosed logical horizon.

- Drain an unfinished legacy accepted horizon under its existing executable and time semantics, suppressing replacements where the existing v1 drain requires it. - The legacy `lastSavedUtcUnix` remains the existing passive-offline source boundary. Tests must cover existing schema chains, legacy active v1/v2 and new-profile reopen/downgrade refusal.

This is a proposed migration **policy**, not an implemented mapping. Review its exact field layout, frozen source validation, timestamp precision, W/`lastSavedUtcUnix` relationship, activation seam and maximum-valid storage envelopes before choosing a target version or writing migration code.

### 10.1 Later offline implementation obligations
