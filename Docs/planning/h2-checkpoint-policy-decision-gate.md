## 6. Safe cuts, failure and lifecycle policy

Retain C (durably closed), A (durably accepted), P (privately closed) and O (actual eligible observation), without another writable scheduler or W. A published cut must bind exact predecessor, complete eligible horizon and its real wall endpoint. A mandatory barrier earlier than O may publish C below A only when the complete accepted obligation is supported and bounded. Never fabricate a wall endpoint for C by proportional interpolation. Private work after a required permanent timestamp cannot become normal continued play before its publication.

H1-F4's failure machinery is the correct foundation: proven rollback retains the predecessor and full observed prefix through F, excludes subsequent known foreground stall, and permits explicit retry; uncertainty retains the exact candidate/predecessor and disposition, blocks progression/actions, and resolves only after fresh authority proof. Commit-before-ack adopts the proven successor once. Runtime, observer and clock acknowledgement must agree before release. No concurrent writer, duplicate wallet/history/journal, per-frame storage retry or speculative continuation is allowed.

This path handles **known persistence failures** today; it does not enforce prospective memory/work/backlog limits. Later H2 must detect headroom before the next indivisible timestamp, invalidate private consumed executions after failed publication, preserve durable A, and define reconstruction from the proven prefix on explicit retry. It must retain eligible pre-F observations while bounding future acceptance. A new overload trigger must use this existing classification path, after an approved distinction between overload and failed IO, rather than create another stall/time authority. Successful slow IO remains eligible under D3 until a real prospective stop observation; genuine background and an unknown post-crash suffix remain distinct.
